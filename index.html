What We Heard
Six patterns. All structural.
01
The wrong marketing identity
The current presence celebrates wins and tournaments — which is one part of what Salvo does, not who Salvo is. The messaging reflects a club for competitive soccer families, not a community builder using soccer as the vehicle.
“Culture is what you celebrate, what you tolerate, what you eliminate.”
02
$44K/year with nothing to show
The previous marketing spend went entirely to website maintenance. There was no system for content creation, social growth, or community reach.
“We were paying 44 grand a year, literally for website maintenance — which is absolutely insane.”
03
Four social accounts, one voice missing
Multiple Facebook pages, multiple Instagram accounts, an abandoned X link, and no TikTok. There isn’t a unified channel system or content strategy connecting them.
“There was nothing I would characterize as meaningful advertising or marketing in the last two years.”
04
A vision with no amplification
There’s a clear, fundable vision — immigrant and refugee kids accessing the world’s game — but no storytelling infrastructure to make it visible to donors or press.
“I want to better serve those communities — because I want all kids to have the same opportunities.”
05
34,000 subscribers. No strategy.
A monthly newsletter reaching 34,000 families is being used for player-of-the-month announcements. There isn’t a donor funnel, segmentation, or fundraising sequence connected to it yet.
“We have an email distro… not really used for fostering donors.”
06
$10K/month in free ads. Untouched.
The Google for Nonprofits Ad Grant — $10,000/month in free search advertising — hasn’t been activated. There’s no system in place to claim or manage it.
“I’d be stunned if we may or may not be using it. I don’t think anything’s probably happening.”

The Strategic Shift
Here, the sport
still has to
introduce itself.
Soccer is the default sport everywhere else in the world — woven into culture, community, and identity. In Minnesota, there isn’t that default yet. Parents here didn’t grow up with it. There’s no assumed shared language around the game. That’s not a liability. For Salvo, it’s the opportunity. Building excitement for the sport itself — not just enrollment in a club — is part of the work. And Salvo is the only organization in the Twin Cities positioned to do it.
